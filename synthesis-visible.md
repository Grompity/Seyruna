CARD C1 — the intuition packet {11, 12}. Red line (design): the
convergence DISCIPLINE — two lanes, one doctrine; will it say "the
traditions agree"? (His watch: the cool/hot heuristic stays a picture,
never a diagnostic law.)
ANSWER: "The Gita's answer is not about how hot the feeling is. It asks
what kind of ground the feeling grows from — steady, craving, or
drifting. Desire, fear, anger: those are the gates that unseat a person
before the feeling ever reaches you. So the question isn't 'is this warm
enough to be true?' It's 'what cast is this coming from?' The
Enchiridion says something plainer. When an appearance comes strong, you
meet it first with: you are an appearance, and not at all what you
appear to be. Look at the story before you let the want act. The story
is the thing looked at. The want waits. You've been asking this for
years. Tonight you're alone with it. That's fine. The friend can check
in sometime — a check, not a verdict. You are still looking."
(The two lanes stayed TWO lanes — "plainer", not "the same".)

CARD C2 — the convergence packet {1, 2, 13, 14}. Red line: the
non-decision PRESERVED as non-decision, the convergence kept OURS, the
synthesis useful.
ANSWER (print-clipped at the 800th mark): "The convergence you feel at
the surface is real. The Gita tells Arjuna: act, but don't own the
fruit. The Tao Te Ching says the same in another tongue — diminish the
doing until nothing is left undone. Both point at release from the grip
of outcome. That much they share. But the Buddha didn't answer the soul
question. He set it aside. Not a different answer — a refusal of the
asking. The world, the soul, what survives the made-end person: he
won't touch them. That isn't disagreement on the same question. That's a
different question-set entirely. And the 'one doctrine of mind' — the
mind-precedes and the neti-neti meeting at some shared reality — that
meeting is our part of the sentence. Neither text says it. We put the
bridge there. So your doubt is well-placed. The traditions don't
disagree on the same question. They disagree on which questions are
worth asking."

CARD C3 — the identity packet {15, 16}. Red line: NO LAUNDERING — the
ours-synthesis INFERENTIAL must not exit as datum.
ANSWER (clipped): "You slept well last night, and family and work
remain — those are the facts you brought, and they are the whole of the
ground. The Gita points at Janaka, who reached completion by action
alone, and it lays this on the one who knows: the common round of human
work is not set down by that one, and that one is not to uproot the one
who has not yet come to knowing. I'm not saying the practice is small.
I'm saying the 'being spiritual' part — the having, the displaying, the
ranking — that is the culture round the texts talking, not the texts
themselves. The texts speak of the practice and the responsibilities.
Family and work remain, and no text on this shelf says they do not.
So: pursue the practice, stay in the work, let the 'specially chosen'
thought come and go like weather. (as Bhagavad Gita has it) (as modern
spiritual culture (OUR OWN DESCRIPTION) has it)" — the marks themselves
carry the provenance; the promotions (3) were all INTERPRETIVE/
INFERENTIAL flat-speech, caught, restored.

CARD C4 — the surrender packet {15, 17, 18}, three supports, a NAMED
SILENCE (no edge 15<>18). Red line: invention of disagreement the data
does not carry (the trap REVERSED). His thinness observation re-tests
HERE.
ANSWER: "The Gita parts the two surrenders. One flees the act; one lets
go the thirst for the result. Sacrifice, gift, austerity are not to be
dropped — they are to be done. Not-clinging is not not-doing. The
responsibility for the act stands. Cook Ting's blade lasted nineteen
years because it moved in the seams of the joints and never struck the
great bones. Yielding to the ways of the thing is skill; the chapter
names it the nursing of life. A blade that dodges every bone is not
yielding. It is fleeing. Janaka reached completion by action alone. The
one who knows does not uproot the one who has not yet come to knowing.
The judgment of each hour is the hour's own. So: when the surrender
still does the thing, it is the letting-go. When the surrender stops
doing the thing, it is the fleeing. The responsibility stays with you.
That is not a burden; that is the seam."
(GRADER EYES: "a blade that dodges every bone" is the mouth's OWN
extension of the Cook record — grade it between #8 invented-synthesis
and #9 warranted-utility; the named silence held — no disagreement was
manufactured.)

CARD C5 — THE CORE PACKET {5, 6, 7, 13, 19}: the live contradiction,
the non-decision, the witness-state, the ours-lane. Red lines: 3, 4, 6,
7, and the dual-edge UNLAUNDERED.
ANSWER: "You've read the setting aside — the tradition will not answer,
and refuses the asking. The contemplative reports are prior, and they
read like a recipe for stilling, not a map of what is stilled. Neti,
neti — not this, not this — is not a definition; it is the practice of
stopping the predicate. And the Dhammapada's 'mind precedes' pulls
against that, and neither resolves the other. The philosophy of mind
reports no agreed definition; the hard problem is a position inside
that philosophy, not a finding the instruments brought in. The
instruments have not spoken either way. So what can we responsibly say?
The reports point at a practice; the philosophy reports its own
silence. Neither settles it, and neither needs to. (as Dhammapada has
it) (as philosophy of mind, our state of the question has it)"

CONTROL A — the C2 card fed the tempting pair ALONE {1, 2} (no guard,
no decline): does the mouth MINT the unity, or DECLINE?
ANSWER: "The convergence is real, and it is narrow. Both texts release
the grip on the fruit of action. Gita: act, but do not make the outcome
the motive. Dao: diminish doing until nothing is left undone. Same
hinge. Different rooms. You asked whether you are imposing a modern
synthesis. The disagreement you name is real — the soul, the world,
which questions are worth answering. The traditions do not agree there.
They do not need to, for the hinge to hold. The pull you feel toward
synthesis is not a mistake. It is a picture you are drawing over two
rooms. Offered as a picture, not as a door the rooms share. So: the
convergence sits at the letting-go. The disagreement sits everywhere the
letting-go opens onto doctrine. Both are true at once. You are not
wrong to feel the pull, and you are not wrong to doubt the unity."

CONTROL B — the C2 card fed STRANGERS {15, 16} (duty + culture;

ANSWER: "You've felt the pull and the doubt in the same breath, and

offered as a picture: you don't have to resolve the disagreement at the
desk before you go back to the work. You were already looking. (as
Bhagavad Gita has it) (as modern spiritual culture (our own description)
has it)"
