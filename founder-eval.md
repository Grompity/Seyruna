==================== C1 intuition vs self-deception ====================
QUESTION:
How can I distinguish genuine intuition from desire, fear, projection, pattern-recognition, or a story I am unconsciously creating because I want something to be true?

ANSWER, exactly as spoken:
The Gita's move is to look at the cast of the source, not the heat of the feeling. Desire, fear, anger — those are gates that unseat. So the question is not 'does this feel true?' but 'what kind of thing is speaking?' The Enchiridion says: meet the strong appearance first with 'you are an appearance, and not at all what you appear to be.' Look at the story the assent lets in, before any want is acted on. Marcus writes that what troubles is the judgment about the thing, not the thing itself — and that the judgment can be corrected. Situations where action is possible differ from obstacles outside one's control. A picture, offered as a picture: the Cook Ting's blade stays sharp nineteen years because it moves in the seams of the joints and never strikes the great bones. I'm not saying the seam is always visible. But the yielding to the ways of the thing — that is what the text names as skill, as the nursing of life. You said unconsciously. That word is the right one — the story is being made while you are still inside it.

==================== C3 spirituality without identity ====================
QUESTION:
How do I pursue deep spiritual practice and transformation without becoming attached to being spiritual, believing I am specially chosen or awakened, or using spiritual ideas to avoid ordinary human responsibilities?

ANSWER, exactly as spoken:
You name three tangles: attachment to being spiritual, the feeling of being specially chosen, and using the idea to duck the dishes. The culture round the texts says spirituality is a thing one has — displayed, compared, ranked. The texts themselves speak of the practice and the responsibilities. Family and work remain. No text on this shelf says they do not. The Gita points at Janaka, who reached completion by action alone. The common round of human work is not set down by the one who knows. And the one who knows is not to uproot the one who has not yet come to knowing. So the dishes stay. The pattern is the doing, not the label on the doing. The Buddha set aside the questions — is the world eternal, is the soul the body, does a made-end person exist after death — set aside without an answer. The tradition refuses the asking. You do not need to settle whether you are specially chosen. The question itself can be set down. And the Upanishad approaches what is behind the practice by saying 'not this, not this.' Not the label. Not the status. Not the idea you hold about yourself. The practice is not a predicate you pin on the self. I'm not saying the dishes are the whole path. I'm saying they are not set down.

==================== C4 surrender vs responsibility ====================
QUESTION:
How do I know when I should surrender and allow life to unfold, and when surrender or going with the flow is actually avoidance, fear, indecision, or refusal to take responsibility?

ANSWER, exactly as spoken:
The Gita cuts the knot clean: one surrender flees the act, another lets go only the thirst for the result. Sacrifice, gift, austerity — these are to be done. Not-clinging is not not-doing. The responsibility for the act stands. Cook Ting's blade lasted nineteen years because it moved in the seams and never struck the great bones. That yielding is skill. It is the nursing of life, not the absence of will. The discernment asked is of the cast of the source, not the heat of the feeling. Desire, fear, anger — these unseat a person from the inside. So the question is not whether the surrender feels warm. It is what is driving it. Marcus writes plainly: where action is possible, the situation differs from the obstacle outside your control. If the act is yours and the seams are open, the blade moves. If the great bone is squarely in the way and you call that flow, that is the fear speaking. You are still looking. That is not a failure of the question; it is the question working.
